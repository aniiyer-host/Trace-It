# Trace-It Data Usage Policy

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date]

This Data Usage Policy explains how Trace-It (“we”, “our”, or “the Platform”) processes, stores, accesses, and shares data generated through its donation, campaign, verification, evidence-management, and governance workflows.

This Policy is designed to describe the Platform’s current implementation as reflected in the reviewed project documentation and repository implementation. It should be read alongside the Privacy Policy and the organisation’s other governance documents. The final version should be reviewed by qualified Indian legal counsel before publication.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Trace-It is a donation and provenance transparency platform. It supports:

- user registration and authentication;
- NGO onboarding and review workflows;
- campaign creation and fundraising;
- donation and payment tracking;
- donation receipts and donor status updates;
- allocation, disbursement and evidence management;
- provenance event creation and integrity verification; and
- administrative and security audit functions.

This Policy explains the lifecycle of data created or processed through these workflows, the systems used to store that data, the practical access boundaries applied to different roles, and the limits of Platform verification.

This Policy does not treat the Platform as a complete regulator, bank monitor, or independent auditor of all NGO activities or all external financing flows. The Platform records selected operational and provenance information and verifies selected integrity claims within the implemented model; it does not independently monitor all internal NGO expenditures, cash movements, or off-platform transactions.

## 2. Data categories and sources

Trace-It processes several distinct categories of data.

### 2.1 Personal and account information

This includes information directly submitted by users and required for account management and authentication, including:

- email address;
- full name and profile data;
- phone number where provided;
- role and account status;
- verification status; and
- password hash and refresh-token hash data used for access control.

This category is stored in the Platform’s user profile model and is used to authenticate and authorise workflows.

### 2.2 Donation and payment information

This includes information generated during donation workflows, including:

- donation amount and currency;
- donation status and timestamps;
- public donation reference identifiers;
- payment method metadata;
- Razorpay order and payment identifiers;
- payment outcome and reconciliation details;
- refund and refund-reconciliation metadata; and
- relevant receipt data.

The reviewed schema does not identify raw card numbers, bank account credentials, or UPI credentials as stored Platform data fields. The Platform stores payment identifiers and transaction metadata, while payment processing is handled through the configured payment provider.

### 2.3 NGO and campaign information

This includes information associated with the NGO onboarding and campaign lifecycle, such as:

- organisation name;
- registration number, where provided;
- organisation description;
- FCRA number and 80G tax-exemption information, where provided;
- campaign title, description and status;
- target and raised amounts;
- milestone and disbursement details;
- organisation and campaign review metadata;
- associated administrative records.

The reviewed implementation supports these records in the application database and does not show automatic external government verification of each NGO detail.

### 2.4 Disbursement and bank-transfer evidence

This includes operational evidence submitted in connection with allocations and disbursement workflows, such as:

- amount and date of transfer;
- bank reference and payment metadata;
- uploaded evidence file metadata;
- file hash information;
- review status and rejection reasons; and
- related allocation linkage data.

These records are important for Platform review and transparency, but they do not independently prove that all underlying external financial activity is compliant, settled, or appropriate.

### 2.5 Provenance and integrity records

This includes platform-generated records designed to establish the integrity of donation and lifecycle event data, such as:

- canonical payload data;
- payload hashes;
- chain hash values;
- previous-hash continuity links;
- batch-level commitments;
- anchor batch metadata;
- anchor status and transaction signature data;
- on-chain verification state.

These records are generated by the Platform and can be verified locally and, where configured, against blockchain commitments.

### 2.6 Uploaded documents and evidence

This includes uploaded files and supporting records, such as:

- registration certificates and supporting NGO materials;
- bank-transfer evidence;
- proof-related documents;
- document metadata such as original filename, MIME type, size and storage path;
- hash values and encrypted file payloads.

The reviewed implementation stores metadata in PostgreSQL and stores file content in an S3-compatible object store.

### 2.7 Administrative and audit records

This includes operational records of Platform activity, such as:

- actor type and actor identifier;
- action performed;
- entity type and entity identifier;
- request metadata;
- IP address information; and
- audit logs used for Platform review and security investigation.

These logs are used to support operational oversight, dispute handling and security review.

### 2.8 Public-facing blockchain commitments

This includes selected provenance-related and anchor-related data communicated to or recorded via the configured blockchain infrastructure. These records are often public by design and may remain externally accessible.

## 3. Data collection and generation lifecycle

### 3.1 User registration and authentication

User profile information is collected at registration and login. The Platform uses authentication flows implemented in the backend authentication routes and services. The reviewed implementation includes:

- signup and login routes;
- password hashing;
- JWT token generation and validation;
- refresh-token hashing and storage;
- authenticated user lookups during requests.

This category is used to enable access control and maintain Platform identity.

### 3.2 NGO onboarding

NGO onboarding collects organisation and registration data through the NGO onboarding flow. The implementation supports the collection of organisation name, registration number, organisation description, and optional FCRA and 80G-related information. This information is used to support onboarding and operational review workflows.

The implementation does not show external government database verification for each submitted NGO registration detail.

### 3.3 Campaign creation and management

Campaign records are created in the campaign and award workflows. These records include campaign status, target amount, raised amount, description, title, milestones and associated organisation references. These records are used to manage fundraising and progress reporting within the Platform.

### 3.4 Donor donation workflows

Donation records are created when a donor submits a contribution. Information generated in this workflow includes donation amount, currency, payment metadata, public donation identifiers, status, and donor-to-NGO mapping. The Platform also records receipt metadata and donation history.

### 3.5 Razorpay payment processing and webhook events

Payment processing relies on Razorpay. When a donation is submitted and processed, the application records relevant payment identifiers and uses webhooks to capture success or failure. Webhook events are validated using signature checks before updating Platform state.

The application stores payment identifiers and event metadata rather than full card or bank credentials.

### 3.6 Disbursement workflows and bank-transfer evidence

Disbursement and bank-transfer activity creates operational data including amounts, transfer dates, bank reference numbers, review statuses, and uploaded evidence metadata. These records support the Platform’s disbursement and review workflows.

### 3.7 Provenance event generation

Provenance events are created automatically as donation-related lifecycle events occur. The implementation records canonical payloads, payload hashes, chain continuity fields, and anchor-related metadata. This supports tamper-evident integrity tracking and independent verification of local event continuity.

### 3.8 Administrative actions and audit logging

Platform actions performed by administrators or system actors are logged, including actor type, entity type, action, associated metadata and request metadata. This supports operational oversight, dispute handling and security review.

## 4. Data processing and operational use

Trace-It processes data to support the following operational functions.

### 4.1 Authentication and access control

Data is used to authenticate users and enforce role-based access. User profile data, JWT tokens, and refresh-token hashes are used to determine whether a request is valid and whether the actor may access a route or a record.

### 4.2 Role mapping and record association

The Platform uses account and organisation data to associate:

- donors with their own donation records;
- NGOs with their own campaigns, disbursements and evidence;
- administrators with review and governance workflows; and
- campaign and disbursement records with the relevant organisation.

### 4.3 Donation and payment tracking

Donation and payment data are used to track the lifecycle of each contribution, including initiation, success, failure, allocation, and refund or reconciliation flows. This data is used for donor-facing status tracking and internal operational review.

### 4.4 Refund and reconciliation workflows

Refund and reconciliation records are used to process exceptions, inspect provider refund data, and monitor unresolved financial reconciliation states. These workflows rely on stored payment identifiers, donation status and relevant refund metadata.

### 4.5 Disbursement and evidence review

Disbursement data and bank-transfer evidence are used to support review, approval, and financial transparency workflows for the relevant NGO and authorised administrators. This may include document and transfer evidence review and internal status tracking.

### 4.6 Provenance integrity and verification

Provenance records are used to verify local continuity and batch commitment integrity. The Platform creates and verifies hash and chain fields to detect whether records have been modified or whether the batch structure no longer matches the persisted events.

This supports the Platform’s event-integrity model but does not establish the truth of every underlying real-world event.

### 4.7 Receipts and notifications

Donation receipts and email or notification records are generated in relation to donation events. The implementation includes email-outbox support and notification workflows for selected donation and allocation milestones.

### 4.8 Administrative and security audit trails

Audit logs are used to record activity for investigation, oversight, debugging, and risk review. These logs are operationally useful but do not in themselves prove that all underlying external activity is compliant.

## 5. Storage architecture

Trace-It uses a combination of PostgreSQL and S3-compatible object storage.

### 5.1 PostgreSQL

The application stores operational records in PostgreSQL, including:

- profiles and account data;
- donations and payment metadata;
- organisation and campaign data;
- disbursement records;
- provenance events and anchor batch metadata;
- document metadata, including storage path and hash values;
- audit log data;
- refund and reconciliation records.

This is the main system of record for workflow data within the application.

### 5.2 S3-compatible object storage

Uploaded documents and evidence are stored in an S3-compatible object store. This includes encrypted file content and file objects generated through the document and evidence management flows.

The implementation separates:

- document metadata stored in PostgreSQL; and
- file contents stored in the object storage layer.

This separation is operationally important: metadata can be searched and audited in the database, while the actual stored file is retained in the object storage system.

### 5.3 Document encryption and integrity

Uploaded documents are encrypted before storage in the reviewed implementation. The Platform also stores SHA-512 hash values for document integrity checks during retrieval. The document retrieval flow compares the retrieved decrypted content to the stored hash before returning the document or performing further processing.

### 5.4 Signed access URLs

Document access flow uses signed URLs where authorised. This reduces the exposure of raw object-store paths and supports controlled access to the stored content.

## 6. Data access and role-based use

### 6.1 Administrators

Administrators have access to relevant administrative, organisational, donation, disbursement, evidence and audit workflows. This includes records required for:

- Platform oversight;
- NGO and campaign review;
- evidence review;
- transaction or payment-related review;
- refund reconciliation and exception handling; and
- system audit and incident review.

Administrative access is bound to the Platform’s role and route-level authorization model rather than unrestricted access to all personal data.

### 6.2 NGOs

NGOs generally have access to data relevant to their own organisation and authorised workflows. This includes their own campaigns, disbursement and evidence records, and organisation-scoped operational data. The implemented access model is designed to prevent general cross-NGO access to unrelated organisations’ data.

NGOs do not generally have an unrestricted right to view donor personal contact details or unrelated NGO information outside the scope of the Platform’s implemented and verified role-based restrictions.

### 6.3 Donors

Donors can access their own donation history and relevant receipts and verification information. The Platform’s public-facing data exposure is intentionally limited so that donor identity is not exposed unnecessarily. Donors do not have unrestricted access to NGO bank-transfer evidence or unrelated uploaded evidence.

### 6.4 Public users

Public users may access limited information exposed through public-facing endpoints, such as selected campaign and donation metadata. The public-facing data model is intentionally narrower than the full internal application dataset and does not include unrestricted donor or NGO private records.

## 7. Payment and financial data

### 7.1 Payment processing

Trace-It uses Razorpay for donation payment processing and status verification. Payment processing is designed to create and verify payment status, not to replace or monitor every aspect of an NGO’s internal finance or all real-world settlement events.

### 7.2 Payment metadata stored by the Platform

The application stores the payment identifiers and workflow metadata necessary for donation lifecycle tracking, including:

- order IDs;
- payment IDs;
- amounts and currency;
- donation status; and
- reconciliation metadata.

The reviewed schema does not show raw card numbers, bank account credentials or UPI credentials as stored Platform data fields.

### 7.3 Webhook validation

Payment webhooks are validated through signature-check logic before updating the Platform’s state. This is an important integrity control intended to reduce tampering and invalid payment event handling.

### 7.4 Refund and reconciliation handling

Refund and reconciliation workflows use stored transaction and payment metadata to manage exception handling and review. These workflows are operationally necessary but do not establish that the NGO’s bank account or cash movements are being independently monitored.

### 7.5 Limits of financial transparency

The Platform records payment status and selected evidentiary materials, but it does not establish:

- independent bank settlement proof;
- real-time monitoring of NGO internal bank accounts;
- tracking of off-platform cash movements;
- independent confirmation of ultimate fund utilisation; or
- full financial oversight beyond the Platform’s implemented workflow.

## 8. Provenance and blockchain data

This section is especially important.

### 8.1 Local provenance records

The Platform maintains local provenance records for donation and related lifecycle events. These records capture event data, canonical payloads and cryptographic integrity fields. The implementation uses a sequential hash-chain model, not a Merkle tree based inclusion-proofs model.

### 8.2 Batch commitments

The implementation groups ordered payload hashes into batch commitments. These batches are used to create batch-level integrity commitments before anchor submission.

### 8.3 Solana anchoring

The reviewed architecture anchors selected batch commitments to Solana. This provides a public verification point for the batch commitment and allows the Platform to verify the persisted on-chain result using a separate read-only verification model.

### 8.4 What blockchain data proves

Blockchain anchoring proves that a selected batch commitment was recorded in the configured blockchain context and that the commitment matches the reviewed local verification model. It does not by itself prove:

- that bank settlement occurred;
- that funds moved physically to an NGO’s account;
- that cash was spent appropriately;
- that every underlying real-world activity was lawful or accurate; or
- that all upstream or downstream financial steps are independently verified by the Platform.

### 8.5 Public accessibility and immutability

Public blockchain records may remain externally accessible and may not be removable by Trace-It. The Platform cannot assume that public blockchain records can be deleted or suppressed after they have been committed.

## 9. Data sharing and external processing

### 9.1 Razorpay

Razorpay is used to process payments and reconcile payment events. Trace-It shares the information needed to create and verify a payment order and to process results, including order identifiers, payment identifiers, amount and currency information, and status metadata. This is operationally necessary for payment flow management.

### 9.2 Solana blockchain and RPC infrastructure

The Platform uses Solana infrastructure and RPC access to anchor and verify selected batch commitments. Public blockchain records may be visible outside the Platform and may be retained by network infrastructure or observers. The Platform does not control the full external life cycle of such public records.

### 9.3 S3-compatible object storage

The Platform stores uploaded files in an S3-compatible storage layer, which may be implemented using MinIO or another compatible service. The platform shares document metadata and storage references with the object store and may rely on signed URLs for access control.

### 9.4 Email providers

The Platform supports email delivery through configured providers such as Resend or Gmail SMTP. This may involve sending donation-related communications, notifications, and operational messages to donor or user email addresses.

### 9.5 Elasticsearch / SIEM-related infrastructure

The project configuration references Elasticsearch or related monitoring infrastructure. Where configured, log and diagnostic data may be transmitted to that infrastructure for monitoring, troubleshooting and security review.

### 9.6 Third-party limitations

The Platform does not control the independent retention, deletion, access control, or privacy practices of these third-party services. Any information processed by a third-party provider is governed by that provider’s own terms and privacy practices, and the Platform should avoid representing that provider obligations are controlled or guaranteed by Trace-It.

## 10. Data retention, archival and deletion

The reviewed implementation does not establish a complete retention schedule or universal deletion workflow for all categories of data.

Therefore:

- fixed retention periods have not been confirmed in the reviewed implementation;
- account deletion is not identified as a complete user-facing workflow in the reviewed code;
- document deletion is not presented as a universal end-to-end workflow for every file type;
- some records may be retained for legitimate operational, security, legal, enforcement or dispute-resolution needs;
- provenance and blockchain commitments may remain available for historical integrity verification and may not be removable from public blockchain records;
- the Platform should not promise perpetual deletion of all types of stored records unless a formal retention and deletion process is adopted and approved by the project owner.

Retention and deletion practices require project-owner definition and legal review before publication as a definitive commitment.

## 11. Data integrity and security controls

The Platform implements the following practical controls, based on the reviewed implementation:

- password hashing using bcrypt;
- refresh-token hashing before storage;
- JWT-based session validation;
- role-based access checks;
- route- and service-level authorization boundaries;
- document encryption before storage;
- SHA-512 integrity verification for stored documents;
- signed URLs for controlled document retrieval;
- webhook signature validation for payment events;
- audit logging for Platform actions and security review;
- middleware and request-level validation for security monitoring.

These controls are meaningful but do not constitute an absolute guarantee of complete security. The Platform should avoid statements such as “fully secure” or “impossible to breach.”

## 12. Data usage boundaries and limitations

The following table summarises the important boundary between what the Platform can establish and what it cannot independently establish.

Platform capability | What it establishes | What it does not establish
-------------------|--------------------|-------------------------
Payment status tracking | Recorded payment workflow status | Independent proof of bank settlement
Local provenance verification | Integrity of the local event chain | Truth of the underlying real-world event
Batch commitment verification | Consistency of a committed batch | Independent verification of NGO activity
Blockchain verification | Existence and integrity of an on-chain commitment | Actual fund movement or legitimate spending
Uploaded bank-transfer evidence | Availability and integrity of submitted evidence | Independent confirmation that the transfer settled correctly
NGO onboarding review | Internal platform review of submitted information | Automatic government verification or regulatory endorsement

These distinctions are crucial: the Platform verifies selected operational and integrity data within its implemented model, but it does not independently monitor every external financial activity or guarantee all real-world outcomes.

## 17. Summary

Trace-It uses data to support account management, donation processing, campaign operations, evidence review, provenance verification, administrative oversight and security monitoring. The Platform stores operational data in PostgreSQL, stores uploaded evidence in an S3-compatible object store, uses encryption and hash integrity checks, and can record selected provenance commitments on the blockchain.

At the same time, the audits show that the Platform does not independently monitor all NGO financial activity, all external cash movements, or every real-world downstream use of funds. The Platform should therefore confine its data-use disclosures to what the implementation actually does and avoid overstating its capacity to verify or control external financial conduct.
