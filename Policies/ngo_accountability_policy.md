# Trace-It NGO Accountability, Regulatory Responsibility and Platform Limitations Policy

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date]

This Policy sets out the allocation of responsibility among NGOs, government and regulatory authorities, and Trace-It in connection with the Platform’s donation, fundraising, verification, evidence-management and governance workflows.

This document should be read together with the Privacy Policy, the Data Usage Policy and the Terms of Use. The purpose of this Policy is to clarify the boundary between:

- organisational responsibility for an NGO’s own conduct, representations and financial management;
- statutory oversight and enforcement by government and competent authorities; and
- the Platform’s own operational responsibilities for software, records, access controls, technical integrity checks, and compliance with its own legal obligations.

The key principle is: Responsibility follows control.

NGOs remain accountable for their organisation’s legitimacy, fundraising statements, financial management, lawful use of funds, project execution, beneficiary-related claims and legal compliance. Government and competent regulatory authorities remain responsible for statutory oversight, inspections, investigations and enforcement under applicable law. Trace-It remains responsible for the Platform’s own operations, the integrity of the records it maintains, and its own legal obligations as a service provider, but it is not a regulator, statutory auditor, or substitute for government oversight.

This Policy is intended to describe the current implementation and operational boundaries as reviewed in the project documentation and repository implementation. It should be reviewed by qualified Indian legal counsel before publication.

## 1. Scope and purpose

Trace-It supports:

- user registration and authentication;
- NGO onboarding and review workflows;
- campaign creation and fundraising;
- donation processing and donation-status tracking;
- disbursement and evidence management;
- provenance record creation and verification; and
- administrative and audit functions.

This Policy does not transform the Platform into a government regulator, a financial authority, a bank, or an independent auditor of every NGO transaction. It does not assume that Trace-It controls all real-world financial or operational decisions made by NGOs or their partners. Instead, it clarifies that the Platform can provide records, verification workflows and operational transparency within the implemented architecture, while leaving legal compliance, fundraising accountability and ultimate fund utilisation to the organisations and authorities that actually exercise control over those activities.

## 2. The principle of responsibility following control

The central accountability principle in this Policy is:

- NGOs control their own organisational status, fundraising statements, governance, internal finances, project execution, and beneficiary-related representations;
- governments and competent authorities control statutory oversight, investigations, inspections, enforcement and legal compliance processes; and
- Trace-It controls the Platform’s own technical operations, data-handling processes, role-based access controls, payment integration, logging, records and verification features within the Platform.

This principle is important because technical controls do not by themselves create legal accountability for underlying real-world conduct. A Platform may keep a record of a donation or a transfer, or may verify a hash chain or a blockchain anchoring event, but it does not thereby assume responsibility for the legality or propriety of the underlying cash movement, internal NGO accounting, or downstream project execution unless it expressly undertakes such responsibility through its own contracts or legal arrangements.

## 3. NGO accountability

### 3.1 Organisational legitimacy and representations

NGOs remain responsible for the truth and accuracy of their organisational representations, including:

- organisation name and legal identity;
- registration details provided during onboarding;
- mission statements and description of activities;
- statements regarding programme objectives, beneficiaries and intended use of funds;
- claims regarding tax status or charitable status, where such status is represented to the Platform or to donors; and
- any representations made in fundraising materials, campaign descriptions, or supporting documents.

The Platform may review submitted information and documents, but review is not the same as independent legal verification of organisational legitimacy, registration status or tax status. Trace-It is not a government registry or a substitute for external government verification.

### 3.2 Fundraising representations and donor communication

NGOs are responsible for the accuracy and legality of all fundraising communications made in connection with a campaign, whether on the Platform or in linked materials. This includes:

- campaign descriptions and objectives;
- projected outcomes or milestones;
- use-of-funds statements;
- timelines and progress claims;
- claims regarding beneficiary impact; and
- any representation that could reasonably influence donor decision-making.

NGOs must not make materially misleading statements, conceal material facts, overstate outcomes, or imply government endorsement or regulatory approval where none exists. Any statement that funds have been used, disbursed, or allocated must be supported by accurate records and actual underlying evidence.

### 3.3 Financial management and lawful utilisation of funds

NGOs remain responsible for their own financial governance and lawful use of funds. This includes:

- maintaining appropriate internal accounting records;
- ensuring that funds are used for the intended organisational or programme purpose;
- complying with applicable Indian law, tax obligations, and organisational governance requirements;
- maintaining proper books and records for receipts, transfers, expenditures and reporting;
- ensuring that any disbursement or transfer represented to the Platform is accurate and supported by evidence; and
- undertaking lawful financial management for all activities associated with the organisation’s fundraising and programme operations.

Trace-It does not independently monitor all NGO bank accounts, internal transfers, cash transactions, off-platform donations, or all downstream expenditure. The Platform cannot verify every internal financial decision made outside the Platform’s operational environment.

### 3.4 Project execution and beneficiary-related claims

NGOs are responsible for actual project execution and beneficiary-related outcomes. This includes:

- delivering the activities or programmes described in a campaign;
- ensuring the proper allocation of funds to intended beneficiaries, programme uses, and operational needs;
- maintaining records sufficient to substantiate project progress and impact claims; and
- clearly distinguishing between actual programme implementation and aspirational or projected outcomes.

Where an NGO makes statements about beneficiaries, impact, services, distributions or outcomes, it remains accountable for those statements. Trace-It may record and present operational evidence and provenance records, but it does not independently verify each beneficiary claim or each downstream project result.

### 3.5 Evidence and disclosures submitted to the Platform

NGOs remain responsible for the truthfulness, completeness and authenticity of documents and evidence uploaded to the Platform, including:

- registration certificates and organisation documents;
- project or campaign evidence;
- bank-transfer evidence;
- disbursement records;
- receipts, invoices and confirmations; and
- supporting documents used in administrative review.

The Platform can store such materials, classify them, and permit authorised review, but the submission of documentation does not eliminate the NGO’s responsibility for the correctness and provenance of the underlying facts represented in those materials.

## 4. Government and regulatory responsibility

Government and competent regulatory authorities are responsible for statutory oversight and enforcement under applicable laws. This includes:

- approval or registration of organisations under the relevant legal framework;
- tax, charity, or non-profit regulation, as applicable;
- oversight of fundraising, charitable activity and compliance obligations;
- inspections, inquiries, investigations and enforcement actions;
- review of filings, returns, declarations and reporting obligations; and
- action against unlawful, fraudulent, deceptive or non-compliant activity.

The Platform does not assume or replace these functions. It is not a government authority, a statutory auditor, or a substitute for official regulatory action. The existence of a Platform account or a recorded donation does not constitute government approval, legal validation, or a guarantee of compliance.

## 5. Trace-It’s responsibilities

Trace-It remains responsible for the technical and operational functions it controls as a platform provider. Those responsibilities include:

- providing and maintaining the Platform’s user account, authentication and authorisation features;
- implementing role-based access and security checks for administrators, NGOs and donors;
- maintaining donation records, payment metadata and related operational logs as configured;
- processing and recording donation status events and payment identifiers within the supported payment flow;
- maintaining audit records, security monitoring features and relevant technical controls;
- enabling evidence-management workflows for uploaded documents and associated metadata;
- maintaining provenance records and verification logic within the implemented model;
- creating or referencing blockchain commitments under the configured provenance and anchoring architecture; and
- discharging its own contractual and legal obligations as a service provider, to the extent applicable under the project’s actual legal arrangements.

Trace-It is not responsible for independently monitoring all NGO finances, external cash movements, other digital or offline transactions, or all downstream uses of funds occurring outside the Platform. Trace-It also does not replace a competent public authority in investigating or prosecuting all forms of misconduct.

## 6. Financial oversight limitations

The Platform is not designed to independently monitor or regulate all financial activity in the real world.

The reviewed implementation does not establish that Trace-It can independently monitor or validate:

- NGO bank accounts or account statements;
- internal cash transactions or cash handling at the NGO level;
- off-platform donations or transfers;
- all downstream expenditure by an NGO;
- all bank settlements or real-time settlement outcomes;
- all external financial records maintained outside the Platform; or
- every instance of potential fraud, misappropriation or misuse outside the Platform’s technical scope.

Therefore, Trace-It cannot and should not be treated as a substitute for:

- an NGO’s internal finance controls;
- external audit or statutory financial review;
- bank monitoring or settlement verification;
- government investigation or enforcement; or
- a regulator’s oversight of the NGO’s lawful operation.

The Platform may provide operational transparency and evidence management, but it does not independently confirm the sufficiency or legality of all underlying financial activity.

## 7. NGO onboarding limitations

NGO onboarding requires the submission of information and supporting documents. The Platform may request or collect:

- organisation name and profile information;
- registration number or related identifiers, where provided;
- organisational description;
- FCRA number and 80G details, where provided;
- uploaded support documents and certificates; and
- review status and administrative metadata.

These details are treated as information submitted to the Platform for onboarding, review and operational administration. They are not the same as independent government verification.

In particular:

- automatic Darpan ID verification is not established in the reviewed implementation;
- automatic CSR registration verification is not established in the reviewed implementation;
- automatic government-registry validation is not established in the reviewed implementation; and
- the Platform’s internal review process does not replace external legal or statutory confirmation of an NGO’s status.

The Platform may display or retain organisation details for administrative and operational purposes, but the final legal validity of an NGO’s status remains with the organisation and the relevant public authorities.

## 8. Donation and payment accountability

### 8.1 Donation initiation

A donor initiates a donation through the Platform’s payment flow. The donor is responsible for making a lawful donation, providing accurate account information where required, and using the Platform in good faith.

### 8.2 Payment processing and recorded status

The Platform records donation amount, currency, payment identifiers, status changes, timestamps and related metadata. The configured payment provider processes the actual payment transaction. The Platform may record whether a Razorpay order or payment is pending, successful, failed, cancelled, or refunded, as provided by the configured payment flow.

### 8.3 Settlement and disbursement

Paid or processed donations are distinct from settlement, disbursement and ultimate fund utilisation. Settlement may depend on the payment provider, the receiving path, and operational arrangements. Disbursement may involve a transfer or allocation of funds as represented by the NGO, with associated evidence uploaded to the Platform.

The Platform does not guarantee:

- the success of every payment;
- the timing of settlement;
- the correctness of all downstream disbursement claims; or
- final utilisation of funds in accordance with any campaign or programme description.

### 8.4 Ultimate fund utilisation

The ultimate use of donated funds remains the responsibility of the NGO and any other party controlling the underlying financial flow. Trace-It does not independently monitor all cash movements, off-platform transfers, or all downstream project expenditure.

## 9. Provenance, blockchain and integrity limitations

The Platform may maintain a provenance model to support integrity verification. Based on the reviewed implementation, the architecture includes sequential SHA-512 hash chaining, a canonical payload model, batch-level commitments and Solana anchoring.

### 9.1 What the implemented provenance model verifies

The implemented model is designed to support evidence integrity and continuity within the Platform’s technical framework. It may verify that:

- records were generated in a continuing order;
- payload data and hash continuity were preserved at the Platform level;
- batch-level commitments were created and recorded; and
- selected records correspond to a blockchain-anchored commitment under the configured implementation.

### 9.2 What it does not prove

The implemented provenance or blockchain architecture does not, on its own, prove:

- that all underlying real-world transactions are lawful;
- that an NGO’s bank settlement occurred as represented;
- that all funds reached the intended beneficiary or project;
- that funds were used in compliance with the campaign description;
- that the underlying cash movement, project outcome, or beneficiary result is authentic in every material respect; or
- that all NGO financial activity was proper, complete or lawful.

This Policy does not claim Merkle-tree proofs, individual inclusion proofs, or any other cryptographic representation that would imply proof of real-world fund utilisation. The implemented system provides integrity and anchoring within the configured technical model; it does not transform that model into a full audit of external finance.

### 9.3 Public blockchain records

Where records are anchored on Solana, the blockchain record may be publicly accessible. Public blockchain records are not private database records. Once committed, a record may remain externally visible and may not be removable by Trace-It. Public accessibility is a technical property of the blockchain network, not a guarantee of legal validity or operational accountability for underlying real-world actions.

## 10. Misconduct, alerts and escalation boundaries

The Platform may detect or record technical alerts, suspicious activity, or operational anomalies. These include:

- failed authentication or suspected account misuse;
- unusual request patterns or access attempts;
- payment inconsistencies or webhook anomalies;
- document integrity or access anomalies;
- suspicious campaign activity or evidence issues; and
- provenance or audit inconsistencies.

These technical alerts are not the same as a finding that a legal violation has occurred. A technical integrity signal may support an operational review, but it does not by itself establish fraud, unlawful conduct, regulatory breach or final legal responsibility.

Similarly, an NGO’s submission of documentation or participation in the Platform does not establish that the organisation is lawfully compliant in all respects. Formal legal findings remain with courts, regulators, law-enforcement agencies, and other competent authorities.

The Platform may restrict, suspend or review access where it reasonably suspects misuse, security risk, or non-compliance with its own operating conditions. That is a technical and operational response. It is not a substitute for external enforcement or legal adjudication.

## 11. Responsibility allocation matrix

The following matrix sets out the responsibility boundaries described in this Policy:

### 11.1 NGO responsibilities

NGOs are responsible for:

- organisation legitimacy and truthful representations;
- fundraising descriptions and campaign accuracy;
- lawful financial management and correct utilisation of funds;
- project execution and beneficiary-related claims;
- submission of accurate supporting evidence;
- compliance with applicable law, tax and organisational obligations; and
- accountability for any conduct outside the Platform that affects the NGO’s operations.

### 11.2 Government and regulatory authority responsibilities

Government and competent authorities are responsible for:

- statutory oversight and registration enforcement;
- inspection, investigation and compliance proceedings;
- tax and charity-related controls, where applicable;
- enforcement of law against fraud, impropriety or misconduct; and
- determining the legal validity of an organisation’s status, actions and compliance under applicable law.

### 11.3 Trace-It responsibilities

Trace-It is responsible for:

- operating the Platform and its technical controls;
- authenticating users and enforcing role-based access;
- maintaining donation and payment metadata as configured;
- maintaining audit logs and security checks within the Platform;
- storing, securing and managing evidence records within the implemented architecture;
- creating and verifying provenance and anchoring records within the implemented model; and
- fulfilling its own legal and contractual obligations as a service provider to the extent they apply.

### 11.4 Shared responsibilities and limits

Some matters are inherently shared or interdependent:

- NGOs and Trace-It may both make statements or provide information to donors or the public;
- Trace-It may provide technical records and evidence access, while the NGO remains accountable for the facts underlying those records;
- regulators may investigate issues arising from Platform data, but the Platform is not the investigator or enforcer;
- donors may rely on a campaign description or a recorded status, but donors remain responsible for their own decision-making and lawful payment activity.

## 15. Conclusion

Trace-It is a donation and provenance transparency platform, not a regulator, statutory auditor, or substitute for government oversight. NGOs remain accountable for their organisational status, fundraising representations, internal financial management, lawful use of funds, project execution and related legal compliance. Government and competent authorities retain statutory oversight and enforcement powers. Trace-It remains responsible for its own operations, the records it maintains, the technical controls it implements, and its own legal and contractual obligations.

Responsibility follows control. Where control lies with the NGO, the NGO remains responsible. Where control lies with a government authority or regulator, that authority remains responsible. Where control lies with Trace-It, Trace-It remains responsible within the scope of its platform operations.

This Policy is intended to reflect the Platform’s actual implementation capabilities and to avoid transferring responsibility that is not properly held by the Platform or by public authorities. It should be finalised only after project-owner review and qualified Indian legal counsel review.
