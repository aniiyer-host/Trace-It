# Trace-It Privacy Policy

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date]

This Privacy Policy explains how Trace-It (“we”, “our”, or “the Platform”) collects, uses, stores, discloses, and protects personal and organisational information in connection with the Platform’s donation, campaign, verification, and governance workflows.

This Policy applies to the three primary user categories on the Platform:

1. Administrators
2. NGOs and charitable organisations
3. Donors and supporters

This Policy is intended to describe the current implementation and operational approach of the Platform as reviewed in the project documentation and source code. The Platform is a donation and provenance transparency platform, not a complete government registry, external regulatory monitor, or full financial control system. The final version of this Policy should be reviewed by qualified Indian legal counsel before publication.

## 1. Scope and purpose

Trace-It is designed to support:

- user registration and authentication;
- NGO onboarding and review workflows;
- campaign creation, fundraising and donation processing;
- donor receipt and donation-status tracking;
- allocation, disbursement and evidence management;
- donation provenance and blockchain verification commitments; and
- administrative audit, risk review and operational oversight within the Platform.

This Policy does not claim that the Platform independently verifies every external financial transaction, independent government registration status, or all downstream uses of funds. The Platform records selected operational and provenance information and provides a structured transparency layer, but it does not replace complete regulatory oversight, independent audit, bank monitoring, or law-enforcement investigation.

## 2. Information we collect

### 2.1 Administrators

Administrators may be provided with access to account, operational and review information necessary to manage the Platform. The reviewed implementation indicates that Administrator-related information may include:

- account and profile information, including email address, full name, phone number, role, verification status and organisational or account status where relevant;
- authentication-related information such as password hashes, JWT-backed session metadata, refresh-token hash storage, and account status information;
- activity and audit records, including action type, entity type, entity identifier, metadata, request identifiers, and IP address information associated with administrative activity;
- records relevant to NGO onboarding, campaign review, donation records, disbursement states, refund jobs, and evidence review;
- request metadata generated by Platform operations or security monitoring processes.

The Platform maintains audit logs for Platform actions and security review purposes. The audit tables and logging logic include actor type, related entity information, action description, metadata payloads, IP address data and request identifiers where available.

### 2.2 NGOs

NGOs may provide and/or generate information through the onboarding, campaign, document, donation and evidence-management workflows. Based on the reviewed implementation, this may include:

- organisation name, profile information and account contact information;
- registration number and organisation description;
- FCRA number and 80G tax-exemption information, where the NGO provides such details;
- account and profile data associated with the NGO’s Platform account;
- uploaded documents, registration certificates and supporting evidence;
- campaign information, including campaign title, description, target amounts, status, milestones, and related disbursement information;
- disbursement and bank-transfer evidence, including amounts, dates, bank references, transfer metadata and uploaded file records;
- review and approval status information generated during Platform moderation or administrative review;
- audit and operational activity records associated with NGO actions and Platform processing.

The reviewed implementation does not show a Darpan ID or CSR registration number field in the current code paths reviewed, and no automatic government database validation is implemented in the reviewed repository. NGO registration details submitted to the Platform are therefore treated as information provided to the Platform for onboarding and review, rather than as independently verified government records.

### 2.3 Donors

Donors may provide or generate information in connection with account creation, donation processing, verification and support. The reviewed implementation indicates that donor information may include:

- account profile information, including email address, full name, phone number, role and verification status where applicable;
- donation amount, currency, payment method, donation status and timestamps;
- Razorpay order and payment identifiers, including order IDs and payment IDs used for payment processing and reconciliation;
- donation message or remarks, where provided by the donor;
- public donation reference identifiers and related donation history;
- tax receipt information and receipt generation metadata where tax receipts are produced;
- provenance and transaction review information tied to a donation;
- audit and operational metadata associated with donation-related Platform actions.

The reviewed application schema stores payment identifiers and transaction metadata rather than raw card numbers, bank account credentials, or UPI credentials. Raw payment instrument details are not identified in the reviewed schema as stored by the Platform itself.

## 3. How we use information

We process information for the following purposes:

### 3.1 Account creation and authentication

We use account and credential information to:

- create and manage user accounts;
- authenticate users using JWT-based access tokens and refresh-token validation;
- enforce role-based access controls; and
- maintain secure login and session flows.

This includes password hashing, refresh-token hashing, and verification of role permissions associated with Administrator, NGO, and Donor accounts.

### 3.2 Role-based access and account management

We use profile and role information to:

- determine access to Platform features and workflows;
- restrict donor access to donor-specific records;
- restrict NGO access to the NGO’s own organisation data and related workflows; and
- enable administrators to access operational and review workflows that require elevated permissions.

### 3.3 NGO onboarding and review

We use NGO-provided information to:

- onboard the organisation to the Platform;
- assess and manage onboarding status;
- review campaign and evidence submissions;
- support verification and administrative workflows;
- maintain audit records of Platform actions undertaken in relation to NGOs.

The reviewed implementation supports NGO onboarding fields such as organisation name, registration number, description, and optional FCRA and 80G information. It does not appear to provide automatic external verification of NGO registration data.

### 3.4 Campaign and donation management

We use donor, NGO and campaign information to:

- create and manage donation records;
- process contributions through the configured payment flow;
- reconcile payment success or failure states;
- update donation status and allocation workflows;
- generate donor receipts and related notifications;
- maintain a complete Platform record of donation lifecycle events.

### 3.5 Payment processing and refund handling

We use transaction metadata and payment identifiers to:

- create Razorpay orders and verify payment success or failure;
- reconcile payment outcomes;
- handle refunds and refund-related exception flows;
- maintain operational records for dispute resolution, review and audit.

### 3.6 Donation receipts, provenance and verification

We use donation and provenance information to:

- generate donor receipts and related confirmations;
- create internal provenance records; and
- maintain selected verification and chain-continuity records associated with donation lifecycle events.

The Platform may create records linked to Solana or related blockchain commitments for integrity checking. These are not the same as independent proof of all real-world financial outcomes.

### 3.7 Disbursement and evidence management

We use documentation, bank-transfer evidence and supporting files to:

- enable NGOs to submit evidence of transfer or proof of need/work;
- allow administrators to review submitted evidence;
- support Platform workflows for disbursement approval, review and monitoring;
- maintain evidence records and associated audit trails.

### 3.8 Security monitoring, fraud prevention and Platform integrity

The Platform uses logs, audit records, security middleware and validation checks to:

- monitor access and activity within the Platform;
- detect or record suspicious or invalid request patterns;
- investigate account issues, payment inconsistencies or unauthorised access attempts;
- maintain operational integrity and support dispute resolution.

The Platform does not represent that it independently detects every instance of fraud, all misconduct, or all external financial abuse beyond the capabilities of the Platform’s implemented controls.

### 3.9 Legal compliance and dispute resolution

We may process information to:

- respond to a request, complaint, dispute or regulatory inquiry;
- support internal review or legal investigation;
- maintain records relevant to the operation of the Platform;
- preserve evidence where required by law, contract, or legitimate operational need.

This does not create a blanket obligation to independently monitor all NGO operations or financial activity outside the Platform’s operational scope.

## 4. Role-based access and visibility

### 4.1 Administrators

Administrators may access Platform data required to operate and review the Platform, including records related to:

- user accounts and roles;
- NGO onboarding and status;
- campaign and disbursement records;
- donation records and payment metadata;
- evidence uploads and bank-transfer records;
- refund jobs and reconciliation cases;
- audit logs and provenance-related records.

Administrative access is intended to be role-based and limited to the functions and records required for Platform administration and oversight.

### 4.2 NGOs

NGOs are generally restricted to their own organisation’s information and associated workflows. In the reviewed implementation, NGOs can usually access:

- their own profile and onboarding information;
- their own campaign data;
- associated donation and disbursement records relevant to their organisation;
- their own uploaded documents and evidence; and
- their own bank-transfer evidence and review workflows.

NGOs are not generally expected to access donor identities or unrelated NGO records beyond clear Platform-authorised contexts. Specific implementation details for cross-organisation access are limited in the reviewed code and should be treated as restricted by default.

### 4.3 Donors

Donors may access information relevant to their own donations, including:

- their own donation history;
- donation status and payment metadata; and
- their own receipts and Platform-provided verification details, where available.

Public-facing Platform information is intentionally limited and should not expose donor identity unnecessarily. Donors do not generally receive unrestricted access to NGO bank-transfer evidence or other uploaded financial documentation beyond the Platform’s implemented donor-facing information flows.

## 5. Third-party services and disclosures

Trace-It uses or may rely on third-party services to operate the Platform. The reviewed implementation identifies the following categories of provider:

### 5.1 Razorpay

Razorpay is used for donation payment processing and payment reconciliation. Information shared with Razorpay may include:

- donation order identifiers;
- payment identifiers;
- amount and currency information;
- payment success/failure status and associated event metadata.

The reviewed implementation does not show that the Platform stores raw card numbers, UPI credentials or full bank account numbers in the application database. However, Razorpay receives payment information required for payment initiation, verification and settlement under its own services and terms.

### 5.2 Solana blockchain

Certain provenance commitments and metadata may be written to the Solana blockchain for integrity verification. Information recorded or referenced on-chain may include selected provenance-related metadata and hash-based commitments created by the Platform’s verification process.

Blockchain records are public by their nature and may be accessible to third parties. The Platform does not guarantee that blockchain records are removable or private. A public blockchain record is not equivalent to a private database record, and blockchain verification does not by itself prove bank settlement, physical movement of funds, or ultimate NGO fund utilisation.

### 5.3 Object storage

The Platform stores uploaded documents and supporting files in an S3-compatible storage layer. The reviewed implementation uses object storage with encrypted content and stored metadata in PostgreSQL. The Platform may share file metadata, storage path information, and file identifiers with the storage provider. This is distinct from the document content itself, which is encrypted before upload in the reviewed implementation.

### 5.4 Email service providers

The Platform supports configurable email delivery via service providers such as Resend or Gmail SMTP, depending on deployment configuration. The Platform may share donor or user email addresses and email content relevant to donation confirmation, receipt generation or operational alerts with the configured email provider.

### 5.5 Elasticsearch / SIEM and monitoring infrastructure

The project configuration references Elasticsearch-based logging and monitoring infrastructure. Where configured, Platform logs and associated metadata may be transmitted to external logging or SIEM infrastructure for monitoring, diagnostics, and operational security review.

### 5.6 Other services and disclosure context

Any third-party service used by the Platform processes data under its own terms, security measures and privacy practices. Where a third-party provider retains or discloses data outside the Platform, that provider’s own practices will govern that processing, and the Platform should not be read as guaranteeing or controlling the provider’s independent retention or data-handling practices.

## 6. Blockchain and public records

The Platform may create provenance records and related blockchain commitments to support integrity verification. These records may be public or externally accessible depending on the blockchain network configuration and the Platform’s operational setup.

Important limitations:

- blockchain records can be publicly accessible;
- once a record is committed to a public blockchain, it may not be removable through Platform control;
- blockchain verification confirms integrity within the implemented verification model, but it does not independently prove bank settlement, physical movement of funds, or ultimate NGO fund utilisation;
- the Platform does not publish personal donor details to the blockchain in the reviewed implementation, and it should not be assumed that all personal data is publicly disclosed merely because a provenance record exists.

## 7. Data security

The Platform implements a number of practical safeguards, based on the reviewed implementation:

- password hashing using bcrypt;
- hashed refresh-token storage;
- JWT-based authentication and role-based access checks;
- restricted access rules for administrators, NGOs and donors;
- server-side validation and API-level checks;
- encryption of uploaded document content before storage using AES-256-CBC in the reviewed implementation;
- SHA-512 hash verification to check document integrity during retrieval;
- signed URLs for access to stored files where applicable;
- HMAC-based webhook signature verification for payment webhooks;
- audit logging and request metadata capture for operational and security review.

These measures reduce risk but do not guarantee absolute security. No system can guarantee that data is invulnerable to all possible attacks, misuse or operational failure. The Platform should therefore avoid absolute assurances such as “completely secure” or “impossible to breach.”

## 8. Data retention, deletion and correction

The reviewed implementation does not establish a final, formal retention schedule for all categories of data. The codebase includes status values for archival and deletion-related document states, but the project does not provide a complete, user-facing retention schedule or a clearly documented account-deletion workflow in the reviewed implementation.

Accordingly:

- no fixed retention period for all personal data is currently confirmed by the reviewed code;
- no self-service account deletion workflow is identified in the reviewed implementation;
- no complete user-facing document deletion guarantee is confirmed for all file categories;
- some records may be retained for legitimate operational, security, legal, compliance or dispute-resolution purposes;
- blockchain commitments may remain publicly accessible and may not be removable through normal Platform controls.

The Platform should adopt and disclose a specific retention policy for each category of personal and operational data before final publication. Until such a schedule is defined, the Platform should avoid promising any specific deletion or retention periods.
